K-Means++ Clustering

KMeans(n_clusters=k, init="k-means++", n_init=10, random_state=42)

Plain K-Means picks its starting centroids uniformly at random, which can occasionally place two centroids in the same dense region and lead to poor convergence. K-Means++ picks the first centroid at random, then each subsequent centroid with probability proportional to its squared distance from the nearest centroid already chosen — spreading centroids out across the data for faster, more consistent convergence.
